Roadmap

Five stages, in order, with the first one still unfinished.

The long-term platform is an AI sales workforce — several specialised agents covering qualification, appointments, follow-up, support and reactivation. None of that is a reason to skip the part that has to work first.

Stage 1 — AI website sales agent. The core conversational sales product. In pre-launch testing now. Current
Stage 2 — AI sales workflow platform. CRM integration, scheduling, automated follow-up, deeper analytics. Months 7–18
Stage 3 — Voice AI. The same qualification logic through a spoken interface rather than a typed one. In development
Stage 4 — AI sales workforce. Specialised agents: qualification, appointments, follow-up, support, reactivation. Beyond year 2
Stage 5 — Enterprise sales infrastructure. Multiple agents across sites, channels and internal workflows. Long term
The first 24 months

What has to be true before any of the rest matters.

Months 1–3 · Product validation

Finish MVP testing, harden the website integration, ship analytics, set initial pricing, interview customers, onboard the first pilots. Measured on active pilots, conversations, qualified leads and whether anyone comes back.

Months 4–6 · Commercial MVP

Introduce paid subscriptions and usage billing, build a real onboarding and support process, get the first integrations in. The point is one piece of evidence: that someone will pay a recurring fee for this.

Months 7–12 · Product-market validation

Grow the customer base, improve answer quality, add CRM integrations, start early voice work, build case studies, measure customer ROI. Retention is prioritised over raw acquisition, deliberately.

Months 13–18 · Scale

First engineering and customer-success hires, wider integrations, stronger security, advanced analytics, expanded voice. This is where the company stops being one person.

Months 19–24 · Enterprise readiness

Enterprise plans, role-based access, audit logging, configurable retention, enterprise onboarding and partner channels — built when enterprise customers actually require them, not before.

Hiring, if it works

2–3 people in year one, 5–7 in year two, growing toward 25–35 by year five — engineering, AI development, sales, customer success, operations. Planning targets, drawn on Philadelphia's existing tech labour market rather than a market that would have to be created.

Capital

One investor, and he has to live with the outcome.

The company is funded entirely by its founder: $50,000 of personal money, committed, for 100% of the membership interest. No investors, no lenders, no grants. That is a real constraint — it is why the first eighteen months concentrate on small and lower mid-market customers, where sales cycles are short, rather than chasing enterprise deals the company cannot yet service.

It is also why the software is built the way it is. There is no leased office, no inventory and no specialised equipment; the product is a website component and cloud infrastructure. The allocation on the left is the plan's own, and like everything else here it is a planning figure that will be corrected by what actually happens.
